OLED True Black

Turns dark-gray page backgrounds into pure black (#000000), so OLED pixels switch fully off. Brightness values below are shown as a percentage of full white (0% = black, 100% = white).

General

Master switch. You can also toggle it anywhere with Alt+Shift+B (change the key on the browser's extension shortcuts page). Also turns thin, dark-gray border lines black. Turn this on if panels still look "boxed in" by a faint frame. Many sites draw card backgrounds, dividers and sticky headers with invisible ::before / ::after layers. This catches those too. Turn off only if something looks broken. Removes blurred, enlarged copies of a video or picture drawn behind the content — e.g. YouTube's "Ambient mode" haze around the player. These are images, not gray backgrounds, so the sliders can't catch them. Remembers which sites had a dark page background and paints them black instantly next time, before the page finishes scanning.
Soft: only near-black grays. Normal: typical dark-mode sites recommended. Aggressive: also lighter grays — more black, but cards and buttons lose contrast against the background.
Black threshold
How light a gray may be and still turn black. The swatch shows the lightest shade that still turns black — it and everything darker become #000. 0% would be pure black only; around 28% (default) covers typical dark-mode grays; 40%+ also takes mid-grays.
Color tolerance
How colourful a dark shade may be and still count as "gray". 0% = pure grays only. Around 9% (default) also takes slightly blue/green-tinted darks like GitHub's. Above ~15% strongly coloured dark areas (navy, dark green) would be turned black too.

Experimental

Where a gray panel stood out from what was behind it, a fine line stays — so you can still see where each panel was, including panels inside panels.
Outline brightness
How bright the outline is. Lower = subtler, higher = easier to see.
Minimum contrast for an outline
A panel only gets an outline if it differed from its surroundings by at least this much. Lower = more outlines (even for faint steps), higher = only clearly visible panels. Buttons, links and list rows light up very faintly when you point at them, instead of staying fully black. Also treats dark grays that are partly see-through (over 60% opaque, normally only over 90%) as backgrounds. Risk: may hide content behind them. Cards that are only a faint white tint (e.g. YouTube's description box, chips, some menus) get a weaker tint, so they sit closer to black.
Overlay strength
How much of the original tint is kept. 100% = unchanged, 30% = faint, 0% = fully transparent (card disappears into black). Slightly darkens pictures and videos, so bright thumbnails don't glare against the black page and static bright spots wear the OLED panel less.
Image brightness
100% = unchanged. 85% is barely noticeable; 70% is clearly dimmer. Shows a small box with the site's original colours (before the extension changed them), where the visible background comes from, whether it turns black (and if not, why), and what is on screen now. Handy for tuning the sliders. Shows exactly which areas the extension detects, in burgundy instead of black.

Disabled sites

    Sites with their own settings

      Set from the toolbar popup: "Active on this site" and "Own settings for this site".

      Browser

      The extension only recolours web pages. The browser's own frame (tabs, toolbar) comes from the optional black theme (a separate package) and the browser's appearance settings.
      Does not work on: browser-internal pages, the built-in PDF viewer, the extension store, the sidebar. For local files, enable "Allow access to file URLs" in the extension details. If a button doesn't open a page, its address is copied — paste it into the address bar.
